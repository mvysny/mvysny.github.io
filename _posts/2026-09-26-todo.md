[DHH's RailsConf 2026 opening speech](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vDjW_dRyKXY)
was indeed unique. An author of Rails, telling the audience
that he stopped programming in Ruby and Rails and moved server
to Rust because it's 99% more effective - not the best
marketing/hyping move. But a very bold move indeed, and
he said things that indeed needed to be said. The age of
hand programming has ended, and the age of agentic development
is here. Or is it?

# Depression vs optimism

[I hate Spring](../java-antipatterns/) and enough has been said.
I was getting depressed about smart people using apparently flawed
way of developing apps, and even the entire Java community
hallucinating collectively that complex is better.

AI lifted that depression and turned it into optimism.
I no longer have to maintain others crap - AI will do it for me.
I no longer have to figure out subtle bean DI configurations
scattered throughout the jars - AI will do that for me.

Gets even better: design patterns that "worked" for humans
no longer will work with AI. The holy metric now is token usage,
and if Spring/JPA glue uses two times more tokens than the simpler approach of barebones JVM
DIY apps glued together with proven frameworks, (I'm not reinventing the wheel here!
I'm just throwing away the garbage that is DI, and enjoying far better architecture as the outcome), then Spring is dead. Not nice to wish others ill, but
I can't deny there's some satisfaction in "I told you so".

# Master the language

With AI, English is the "new" programming language. And so
it's not that I can throw mediocre prompts in broken English at
the AI and expect marvelous outcome - It's still worth to master
the language, even though the language is now English.
